# Session record: milestones in Running Lean, 2026-10-22

## Starting point

- **Already knew:** The user had just started reading *Running Lean* (3rd edition, Ash Maurya) with friends, who recommended it. The group had filled in about 10 Lean Canvases, but the user knew nothing else about the book's ideas.
- **Wanted to understand:** The milestones the book describes.

## Questions and conclusions

### What are the milestones in Running Lean?

- **Understood:** In the 3rd edition, a milestone is a traction target, not a product feature. Traction is the rate at which a business model captures monetizable value from its customers. The book prefers a traction roadmap, which sets outcomes, over a product roadmap, which sets outputs. The roadmap is built backwards. First set minimum success criteria: the smallest result, about 3 years out, that would make the project worth doing. Then convert that result into a customer count and work back at roughly 10x per year. In Maurya's example, $10M a year at $200 a month needs about 4,167 customers in year 3, about 416 in year 2, and about 42 in year 1. The first explanation covered too much at once and the user did not follow it. A simpler version helped: a milestone is a number of customers to reach by a certain date, such as 10 paying students by the end of year 1 for a tutoring app whose 3-year goal needs 1,000.
- **Check:** covered but not checked. The user did not answer a request to explain the division by 10 in their own words.
- **Sources:** [InfoQ interview with Maurya](https://www.infoq.com/articles/scaling-lean-ash-maurya) (definition of traction, minimum success criteria, the 10x example); [O'Reilly preview of chapter 4](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/running-lean-3rd/9781098108762/ch04.html) (traction roadmap versus product roadmap).

### Are milestones only customer counts?

- **Understood:** No. There are two kinds. Customer-count milestones come from the traction roadmap. Stage milestones are named after what has been proven. The book calls Problem/Solution Fit "the first significant validation milestone", the point where enough demand has been shown to justify building. Product/Market Fit is stage 2, roughly 1 to 24 months later. The counts show how far you have got, and the stages name what you have proven. A customer means someone who pays or commits, not someone who only signs up. Whether the book calls stage 3 "Scale" was not checked.
- **Check:** The user confirmed that this answered the question. They did not answer a follow-up about whether a waitlist with no payments counts as Problem/Solution Fit.
- **Sources:** [O'Reilly preview of Part III](https://oreilly.com/library/view/running-lean-3rd/9781098108762/part03.html).

### What would these milestones look like for an indie iOS or game developer?

- **Understood:** The book gives no indie examples, so this was the agent's own application, with made-up numbers. For a subscription iOS app at $5 a month, a 3-year minimum of $60k a year needs 1,000 subscribers, giving milestones of 10 and then 100. Problem/Solution Fit evidence would be beta users who keep coming back and some who pay or pre-order. Product/Market Fit evidence would be subscribers who keep paying and new users arriving by word of mouth. For a premium game, milestones fit better per release than per year. Wishlists and waitlists are weak evidence because they cost nothing. Returning playtesters and paid early access are stronger.
- **Check:** covered but not checked.
- **Sources:** none; inference.

### Does 10, 100, 1,000 still make sense for indies?

- **Understood:** Maurya presents 10x per year as a rule of thumb for fast-growing businesses and calls the roadmap a rough estimate to revise. For indies the method still holds: start from your own minimum, which may be modest, and keep the first milestone small enough to test soon. The growth factor can be smaller, such as 3x, as long as the numbers still reach the 3-year minimum. Games are better planned per release. The part beyond Maurya's framing is the agent's judgment.
- **Check:** covered but not checked.
- **Sources:** [InfoQ interview with Maurya](https://www.infoq.com/articles/scaling-lean-ash-maurya).

### Do game sales really spike at launch, and can the data be plotted?

- **Understood:** For the typical Steam game, yes. For 2023 Steam releases, GameDiscoverCo found median first-year revenue of 2.64x first-week revenue. Other samples found 3.16x and 3.05x. So the first week earns roughly a third of the first year. Outliers such as Lethal Company (507x) grew mostly after launch. Games that earned under $1,000 in week one grew about 6x, a smaller relative spike. The public data gives only two checkpoints (week 1 and year 1), not a curve, so it can't support an honest sales-over-time plot. Real curves would come from developer postmortems or estimate sites.

- **Sources:** [GameDev Reports, republishing GameDiscoverCo](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-games-long-tail-revenue); [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2021/09/23/how-games-first-week-revenue-on-steam-correlates-with-year-one-revenue). Both are secondary reports; the originals were not read.
