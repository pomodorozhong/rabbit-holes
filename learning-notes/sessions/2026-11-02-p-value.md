## Questions and conclusions

### What probability does a p-value measure?

- **Covered:** A p-value counts outcomes at least as extreme as the observed result, under a specified null hypothesis and model assumptions. It does not generally equal the probability of the exact outcome or the probability that the hypothesis is true.
- **Examples:** Ten fair-coin flips illustrated a two-sided test. Five multiple-choice questions illustrated an upper-tail test: four or five correct under independent uniform guessing has probability 1.5625%.
- **Check:** The user found the initial explanation unintuitive and asked for a different example after an unfamiliar description of uneven coin results. Examples were explained, but no restatement or prediction established understanding. The coin distribution and quiz arithmetic were computed directly.
- **Sources:** [ASA statement](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf).

### How does a scientific paper use the result?

- **Covered:** A hypothetical teaching-method comparison illustrated testing a no-difference assumption. The five-point difference and p = 0.02 were illustrative, not measured or calculated from a supplied dataset. Statistical significance should be considered alongside effect estimates, confidence intervals, and study design.
- **Check:** The user moved to an application without completing the earlier comprehension question. The interpretation guidance was checked against primary sources.
- **Sources:** [ASA statement](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf); [Cochrane interpretation guidance](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-15).

### How could a gacha result challenge an advertised 2% rate?

- **Covered:** For a prespecified sample of 300 independent pulls with a constant target-character rate, a lower-tail exact binomial test compares observed counts against 2%. Zero copies gives p ≈ 0.00233; one gives p ≈ 0.01661; two gives p ≈ 0.06018. Thus zero or one is significant at a chosen p < 0.05 threshold. An observed one-copy rate is about 0.33%; this does not prove intentional rigging.
- **Design limits:** Include all eligible pulls, avoid selecting only unlucky reports, and keep the planned stopping point. A pity system or a different advertised-rate definition requires a different model. The sample size does not guarantee significance.
- **Check:** Binomial probabilities were calculated in JavaScript. No game's actual drop claim, mechanics, or pull dataset was supplied. User understanding has not been confirmed by a prediction or restatement.
- **Sources:** [SciPy binomial-test documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.binomtest.html); [Psychonomic Society statistical guidelines](https://featuredcontent.psychonomic.org/psychonomic-society-statistical-guidelines-updated/).
