# Famous Taverns and Inns of the Multiverse

**Source:** Synthetic clip · D&D trivia · POC fixture

Every adventuring party remembers where they bought their first round. These taverns appear across official adventures, novels, and community lore.

## The Yawning Portal (Waterdeep)

Built around a gaping shaft into the Undermountain dungeon. Patrons can pay to be lowered on a winch into upper levels of Halaster's mega-dungeon. The ground floor serves ale; the basement serves legend.

**Signature drink:** Undermountain Ale (reportedly brewed with water from the first landing).

## The Prancing Pony (Bree)

Technically from Tolkien's Middle-earth, but endlessly referenced at D&D tables. A crossroads inn where strangers overhear dangerous news. If your DM names the local inn "The Prancing Pony," expect at least one hooded ranger.

## The Inn of the Last Home (Krynn)

From Dragonlance. Home of Otik's spiced potatoes and the Heroes of the Lance origin story. A warm common room contrasts with world-shaking wars outside.

## The Rusty Dragon (Phandalin)

Featured in the *Lost Mine of Phandelver* starter adventure. A rebuilt frontier tavern where adventurers pick up rumors, meet NPCs like Toblen Stonehill, and dodge Redbrand ruffians.

## The Bloated Calf (Ravenloft-adjacent trope)

Not one canonical pub — but every gothic horror domain seems to have a rain-slicked inn with a suspicious innkeeper. Candles burn low; the meat is questionable; the tab is eternal.

## Why taverns matter in D&D

- **Quest hooks** arrive over ale
- **Short rests** happen between rounds
- **Party formation** often starts at the bar

**Trivia:** In 5e, a pint of ale costs 4 cp in the Player's Handbook equipment list. A night at a good inn is 7 sp.
