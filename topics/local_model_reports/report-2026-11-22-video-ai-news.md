## Worth trying on your Mac

This week's best local releases are small tools rather than chat models. **Depth Pro 2** from Apple estimates distance in every pixel of a photo or video, and its small size runs in real time on a Mac. **Voice Isolator** from Inworld removes background noise from speech with a 0.26 GB model. **SenseNova-Loop-9B** is a compact image-and-text model whose 4-bit file fits, but it needs a forked build of llama.cpp.

### Depth from photos and video

**Depth Pro 2** predicts, for each pixel, how far that point is from the camera in meters. It works on a single photo, a whole video, or a live camera feed, and keeps the depth steady from one frame to the next. Apple released four sizes: S (24M parameters, 97 MB), B (97M, 386 MB), L (334M, 1.34 GB) and H+ (872M, 3.49 GB), plus a 94 MB focal-length estimator for photos without camera data. The command-line tool picks CUDA, Apple's Metal backend (MPS) or the CPU automatically. The repository also includes a small Mac app that shows live depth from the camera using Core ML.

Apple's demo notes give the most useful guidance for this machine. Memory grows with frame size: S needs about 1.5 GB at 854×480 and about 5 GB at 1280×720, while L needs about 18 GB at 1280×720. The notes say that on a 16 GB Mac you should keep to S or B. On an M4 Max, S runs at 58 frames per second at 854×480; an M2 will be slower, but S should still be usable for live video at small sizes. The command-line default is H+, so start with `-m depthpro2-s` as the README suggests for laptops. The weights are licensed for non-commercial research only. [Project](https://apple-aiml-research.github.io/ml-depth-pro-2/) · [Repository](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-depth-pro-2) · [Mac demo notes](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-depth-pro-2/blob/main/demo/README.md) · [Weights](https://huggingface.co/apple/DepthPro2) · [Video chapter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bj3JTd-ezYg&t=241s)

### Cleaning up speech

**Voice Isolator** removes noise, music and other voices from a speech recording while keeping the speaker's own voice. It works on 48 kHz audio. The offline checkpoint (64.0M parameters, 0.26 GB) gives the best quality for files; the realtime checkpoint (58.2M, 0.23 GB) processes a live stream with 20 ms of look-ahead. It is a useful step before a speech recognizer such as Whisper when the recording is noisy. The package runs on MPS with PyTorch 2.6 or later, and falls back to the CPU otherwise. Inworld tested it on NVIDIA data-center GPUs and on CPU, not on a Mac, so whether the realtime checkpoint keeps up with live audio on an M2 is unknown. Code and weights are Apache-2.0. [Announcement](https://inworld.ai/open-source#voice-isolator) · [Repository](https://github.com/inworld-ai/voice-isolator) · [Weights](https://huggingface.co/inworld/voice-isolator) · [Video chapter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bj3JTd-ezYg&t=924s)

### Compact multimodal chat

**SenseNova-Loop-9B** from SenseTime reads images and text and writes text. It gets extra depth from a small model by running 24 of its 32 layers twice, so each token passes through 56 layers while only about 9B parameters are stored. SenseTime reports scores close to Qwen3.8-27B on agent and data-analysis tasks (65.31 against 65.01 on AIDABench, 41.36 against 44.60 on NL2Repo). These are the publisher's own results.

The official GGUF repository has a 5.82 GB Q4_K_M file and a 0.92 GB image projector, 6.74 GB together. SenseTime's memory table for 4-bit weights gives about 9–10 GiB at a 64K context, so a short context such as the README's 4,096 tokens should leave headroom. The catch is the runtime: stock llama.cpp does not know this looped architecture, and the README points to a pinned commit of the [OpenSenseNova llama.cpp fork](https://github.com/OpenSenseNova/llama.cpp/tree/24ea7bb78410e0f0f7bbb362e37c60cd9ecfc761). Whether the fork's new operations run on Metal was not checked, so it may fall back to the CPU. The model is tagged for English and Chinese; Traditional Chinese is not mentioned. Weights are Apache-2.0. [Announcement](https://www.sensetime.com/en/news/sensenova-loop-9b-release-20261118-1435) · [GGUF weights](https://huggingface.co/sensenova/SenseNova-Loop-9B-GGUF) · [Video chapter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bj3JTd-ezYg&t=1734s)

## Other developments worth knowing

- **Claimed proofs of two famous problems.** A note posted at alpo.ge, whose timeline ends on November 16, says its author and Claude found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis and its generalized form, and formalized it in Lean. Only the Lean files are released, as 14 archive parts; the author writes that there is no readable paper yet and that they have not fully mastered the argument. A separate repository by L. Alpöge and T. Buckmaster claims finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations, which would settle that Millennium Prize problem in the negative. Its 79-page paper and Lean 4 formalization are public, but the README notes that a numerical certificate and its interval-arithmetic check are not included, and that the patched package was not rebuilt. This review found no independent check of either claim. [Riemann note](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Navier–Stokes repository](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/)
- **New hosted frontier models.** OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra ($8 / $40 per million input / output tokens) and the cheaper GPT-6.2 Sol ($2 / $10). Google released Gemini 4 Neon ($3 / $15, 1M-token context), a mid-tier model aimed at coding and long agent tasks. Tencent's Hy4 flash (224B total, 12B active) is very cheap through its API ($0.07 / $0.28) and is open-weight, but far too large for this Mac. [GPT-6.2](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/) · [Gemini 4 Neon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/) · [Hy4 flash](https://hy.tencent.ai/research/hy4-flash)
- **WeMM-Video** from Tencent understands video together with its soundtrack. The 2B and 4B GGUF bundles are 5.34 GB and 8.11 GB, which would fit, but they need Tencent's own native runtime, and its build guide covers only Linux. Treat it as a near-miss until a Mac build is confirmed. [Repository](https://github.com/Tencent/WeMM-Video)
- **Large open models that do not fit.** Maxwell-V1-Flash (35B total, 3B active) has community GGUF files, but the smallest is 17.8 GB and they need a patched llama.cpp. Sage S2.1 Flash from Shanghai AI Laboratory is a 320B science agent model. Bria RMBG 3.0 removes image backgrounds with an 8B editing model that needs 32 GB of GPU memory; the older [RMBG-2.0](https://huggingface.co/briaai/RMBG-2.0) (0.2B, ONNX files from 0.23 GB) remains the small choice.

### Deployment evidence

The machine usually has **8–11 GB** available for weights and working memory after macOS and other applications. A file's size is only the starting cost: depth models also hold frame buffers that grow with resolution, and language models hold a cache that grows with the context. The starting points above are suggested first trials, and peak memory and speed on the target M2 are unmeasured. [Shared hardware assumptions](./memory-budget.md)

### Other chapter notes

- **Why WeMM-Video stays a near-miss.** Tencent ships each size in BF16, FP8, AWQ, NVFP4 and a three-file GGUF bundle (language model, image encoder and audio encoder). The 4B bundle is 8.11 GB and the 2B bundle 5.34 GB. The native runtime that reads these bundles is built "on Linux" with a CPU or CUDA backend, and a Mac build is not documented. The 2B BF16 checkpoint (6.59 GB) might run through Transformers on MPS, but that was not checked. The model is described as Chinese–English bilingual. [Native runtime](https://github.com/Tencent/WeMM-Video/blob/main/native/README.md) · [4B GGUF](https://huggingface.co/tencent/WeMM-Video-4B-GGUF)
- **Why Maxwell's GGUF files do not help here.** The community uploader that wrote Maxwell's llama.cpp support reports that this model loses more accuracy to quantization than most, and declined to publish files under 20 GB because their quality dropped sharply. The smallest file from another uploader, 17.8 GB at Q3_K_M, still exceeds the 11 GB limit. [AtomicChat GGUF](https://huggingface.co/AtomicChat/Maxwell-V1-Flash-GGUF)
- **The mathematics chapters.** Both claims come with Lean formalizations, which can check that a proof follows from its stated definitions but cannot by themselves confirm that the statements match the intended problems. The Riemann note gives a dated timeline of the work and credits Claude with key steps; it releases the Lean archive only. The Navier–Stokes repository says its Lean statements are proved from a 600,000-line library, but the computer-assisted step relies on interval arithmetic outside the formalization, and that certificate is not yet released.
- **Video and avatar research.** LongCat-Video-Avatar 1.6, FastFrames, MovieRoll, Duo and ReDiverse all build on video models of 1.3B to 14B parameters or more, with CUDA-only code. They are useful as research directions rather than local tools for this machine.
